# Co-Walk Context
Co-walk is a multiplayer VR solution, the main focus is to enable 2 players to walk together in VR. These types of solutions fall into the “Social VR” category of solutions, where the main game loop is socializing and having fun without a strict mechanic to define that. 
## Challenge One: Locomotion in VR
Typical VR solutions rely on artificial methods to allow users move around, this is mainly achieved through joystick movements or by teleporting from one point to the other. These methods being artificial break two important factors in VR, Factor one:
Cybersickness, ultimately when experiencing movement in real life, your brain gives the order to consume energy to move your muscles, the energy is consumed and in sync it (the brain) is given feedback from the eyes, ears, skins sensors that confirm the movement! In VR if you just push a joystick to move forward and then you are given the feedback of “you moved forward” your brain will not really like that because it didn’t really consume (or perceive that it consumed) the proper amount of energy to move forward, this dislike on the brain side will cause side effects like Nausea, this discomfort is called Cybersickness!
## Challenge Two: Maintaining Social Presence
You can read about social presence a lot in research, it mainly defines the parameters that make us perceive and interact socially with each other, things like perception of oneself, other and the dynamics of these perceptions when in a social context. This social presence thing, mainly how we perceive others is not really preserved when one teleports from point A to point B, mainly because we don’t do that in real life (unlucky). 
So we define the problem, how to fix it? (why should we fix it, is for you to decide)
# Design Features
## Armswinging 
Arm-swinging locomotion is achieved by tracking the controllers’ movement and computing movement vectors. The vectors are projected forward so that the front swings are more effective at moving the user than the side swings. For the direction of movement, and to enable moving forward while looking to the side, the system tracks the body and predicts the position and orientation of the user’s hips, which define the direction of movement. Since other gestures, such as pointing at something, could collide with arm-swinging for locomotion, users must touch their controllers (detected by capacitive sensors) to enable arm-swinging.
## Aura: A visual represenation of Separation
To improve the perception of the separation distance between users, we implemented a continuous visual cue that reifies3 it. This aura provides users with information on how separated they are from each other. Its goal is to extend the perception of the partner in case participants could not see each other while walking together, by visualizing the separation distance as they move. The Aura is represented as a circular pulse on the ground. It takes the metaphor of walking on water: when moving, the pulse starts from the feet and expands around the user, but when standing still it disappears. A user only sees the pulse from their partner and not their own. If the partner is moving while inside the user’s field of view then their pulse is not visible. The pulse is circular, transparent on the inside but colored at the border. The color of the border changes based on the separation distance. At close proximity (below a certain threshold), it is light gray, but as the separation distance increases it starts shifting to become orange and then red at maximum proximity. Above a certain maximum value, the pulse is not visible, and will not reappear until the pair rejoins.
